<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Remember when tech leaders would tell their employees to <a href="https://www.businessinsider.com/mark-zuckerberg-innovation-2009-10?r=US&amp;IR=T">“move fast and break things”?</a> It seemed that would be the way of AI too. But after a summer where rogue AI agents became reality, and researchers warned <a href="https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/991927/anthropic-ai-kill-all-humans">that AI could kill us all</a>, a number of leading US AI companies are publicly suggesting it’s time to pump the brakes and “pace the frontier” of bleeding-edge AI development.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><a href="https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/995186/is-big-techs-ai-slowdown-a-safety-pact-or-a-cartel">Their motivations are suspect</a>, but leaders at major AI companies — including Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, and X — are at least paying lip service to the idea of a superintelligence slowdown.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Will these AI companies actually slow down? Will anyone step in to regulate these companies like <a href="https://www.theverge.com/policy/995534/a-brief-history-of-ai-executives-calling-for-regulation">they claim to have wanted for years</a>? Will they manage to convince world leaders that the US must “beat China” to superintelligence and go even faster?</p>
