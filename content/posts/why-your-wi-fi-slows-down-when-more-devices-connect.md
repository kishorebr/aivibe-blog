                Demands on home Wi-Fi have increased considerably over the past decade, never mind the launch of the first routers way back in the 1990s. It used to be that homes had a handful of connected devices at most, typically big-ticket items like PCs and game consoles. Today, there's a good chance your watch is online too, and that you've got a plethora of cool smart home gadgets ranging from lamps to smart speakers.
That can create a real crunch on performance. But why is that, exactly? You probably know part of the answer, yet there may be more going on than you imagine. Your Wi-Fi router isn't just a series of tubes — a better analogy might be a central train station with complicated lines of incoming and outgoing traffic.
