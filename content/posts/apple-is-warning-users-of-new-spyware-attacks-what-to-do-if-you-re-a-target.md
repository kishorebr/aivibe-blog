    Apple is warning users of new spyware attacks - what to do if you're a target
     
    New spyware attacks are aimed at journalists, activists, politicians, diplomats, and others. Here's how Apple is notifying them and what they should do - after turning on Lockdown Mode.
      Written by 
            Lance Whitney, ContributorContributor  Aug. 14, 2026 at 9:08 a.m. PT                            Lance Whitney/ZDNETFollow ZDNET: Add us as a preferred source on GoogleZDNET's key takeawaysApple is warning certain users about targeted spyware attacks.These attacks are aimed at journalists, activists, politicians, and diplomats.Anyone notified should enable Lockdown Mode on their iPhone, iPad, or Mac.Apple is warning certain users about a new round of targeted attacks designed to infect their iPhones, iPads, and Macs with what it calls mercenary spyware. If successful, the spyware is able to bypass any built-in encryption to access your private files, snoop on your conversations, capture your audio and video, track your locations, and control your device.Also: Older iPhones have an unfixable security flaw - why it can't be patched and the models affectedSharing the latest details with TechCrunch, Apple said that it sent the notifications to select customers on Thursday across 110 countries. The spyware is dubbed mercenary because it's typically carried out by, or deployed on behalf of, nation states. Further, the attacks aren't aimed at the average person but rather at people with prominent roles in society, including journalists, activists, politicians, and diplomats. Still, if you're a victim, the damage can prove devastating."Such attacks are vastly more sophisticated than regular cybercriminal activity, as mercenary spyware attackers apply exceptional resources to target a very small number of specific individuals and their devices," Apple said in an updated support page about its notifications.
