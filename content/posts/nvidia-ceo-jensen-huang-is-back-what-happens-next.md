<p class="wp-block-paragraph">After the leather-jacketed CEO, president, and co-founder of Nvidia, Jensen Huang, <a href="https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1029595/full-text-jensen-huang-affair-memo-nvidia">acknowledged an affair</a> with Megaspeed exec Alice Huang, his company initially announced an investigation. However, after <em><a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/11/technology/jensen-huang-nvidia-affair.html">The New York Times</a> </em>reported there was evidence of another affair, this time with an Nvidia executive who reported directly to him, a board meeting <a href="https://www.theverge.com/tech/1031652/jensen-huang-rumor">that night</a> ended with <a href="https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1031689/jensen-huang-nvidia-ceo-resigns">Huang’s resignation</a>, and Nvidia CFO Colette Kress was installed as the new interim CEO.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">And then… Huang was back. </p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">In recent years, Nvidia has become a key part of the Trump administration&#8217;s AI push, a central figure in negotiations over potential Chinese access to cutting-edge AI hardware, and a company that has used its now $4 trillion-plus market cap to finance other AI companies. Saying it received input “from senior levels of the United States government,” Nvidia’s board announced his return as CEO on November 23rd, less than two weeks after his resignation.</p>
