                Apple has unveiled a new research study with the aim of learning how people use AI health tools in order to make them more effective and user-friendly. The company is launching the Health AI Usage Study in conjunction with OpenEvidence, a clinical AI platform used by health professionals that draws medical data from sources like The New England Journal of Medicine. 
A key part of the research is a new app now available for download, with Apple aiming to have up to 200,000 participants. Users must live in the US and be 21 or older, and have an iPhone 15 Pro or later model running iOS 26 and up. They can choose to share (or not) Apple HealthKit data, including activity levels, sleep quality and lab results, for extra context around their health.
