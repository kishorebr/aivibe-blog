ZDNET’s key takeaways 




Gen Z has the least interest (and finds the least use) in AI at work. 



A survey highlights how the generation’s AI opinions differ from Millennials, Gen X, and Boomers.



Gen Z increasingly faces a tough job market. 








Generation Z might have the lowest morale around AI at work compared to other generations. That’s according to a survey from talent services firm Seramount, which found that Gen Z was the most AI-averse among the generations surveyed about using the technology in their jobs. 



Gen Z, which includes those born between 1997 and 2012, expressed the least interest and found the least utility in AI, Seramount found. Only 18% of Gen Z reported an interest in strengthening their AI skills, compared to 36% of Millennials, 46% of Gen Xers, and 43% of Boomers. Gen Z is also using AI less than other generations, by nine percentage points.

			More from ZDNET
		
			





“Employers cannot assume their youngest employees will embrace AI on their own,” said Diana Forster, senior director of research at Seramount.



The survey polled responses from over 1,800 US white-collar workers at companies with at least 500 employees. 



Around half of Gen Z report weekly AI use (not specific to work), according to a survey from Walton Family Foundation, GSV Ventures and Gallup, but the generation’s skepticism has mounted over the past few years. Even members of Gen Z who use AI daily have less positive views on the technology. In the same study, 42% of Gen Z reported feeling anxious about AI in 2026, a 1% increase from 2025. Last year, 36% of Gen Z respondents reported excitement about AI. In 2026, that percentage dropped to 22%.
