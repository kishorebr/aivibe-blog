                Have you ever counted up everything in your home that connects to the internet? There's your phone and laptop, but also the TV and game console, plus maybe a video doorbell and many other smart home devices. It's pretty likely that they're all sharing one public internet address, and it's your router's job to figure out which incoming data belongs to which device. This is because IPv4, the internet's original address system, ran out of fresh addresses back in 2011.
It was replaced by IPv6, which has enough addresses for all the devices in the world to have their own (several trillion times over!) According to Google's IPv6 page, roughly half of its users connect via IPv6, and most of the routers you'll come across today support it alongside IPv4 in a setup called dual stack.
