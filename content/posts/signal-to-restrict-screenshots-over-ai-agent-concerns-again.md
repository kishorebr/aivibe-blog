ZDNET’s key takeaways 




Secure messenger Signal is disabling screenshots.



The move is in response to an uptick in AI agents from major OS developers.



Agents can undermine Signal’s mission to keep chats private. 








Privacy concerns around AI agents have prompted encrypted messaging app Signal to set new limits on screenshots. The company will prevent screenshotting in its iOS 27 app and “when AI assistants attempt to capture screen data on Android,” Signal President Meredith Whittaker wrote in a blog post Friday.



Whittaker noted that Google and Apple’s agentic features, including Gemini Intelligence and Siri AI’s Onscreen Awareness, compromise the privacy missions of third-party developers. These features observe what’s on a user’s screen as context for completing requests.

			More from ZDNET
		
			





“Your sensitive data is being captured, stored, and made accessible to whatever local database or remote server an OS-level AI agent is using at that moment,” Whittaker said. “All of this is outside of Signal’s control once the data has been captured, and imperils the privacy of all the participants in a conversation.”



Signal also blocked screenshots last year in response to Microsoft’s controversial Recall AI feature, which could capture screenshots of Signal chats on devices running Windows 11. 



More agents, more risks



Signal’s move is another reminder of how Big Tech’s AI strategy trickles downstream. As AI features have moved from services external to the OS (like chatbots from OpenAI or Anthropic) to further inside of it, third-party apps have to adapt to conditions that undermine their hard-won privacy infrastructure.
