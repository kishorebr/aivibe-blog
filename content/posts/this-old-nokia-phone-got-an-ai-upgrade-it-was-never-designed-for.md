                Nearly 20 years ago, Nokia released the Nokia 6300. Despite arriving the same month Steve Jobs would introduce the iPhone at MacWorld 2007, the 6300 would go on to become one of Nokia's best-selling phones, thanks to its understated but attractive design, affordable price and easy-to-use software. In 2026, however, the original 6300, not to be confused with the recent 4G rerelease, is a device on borrowed time.
For one, it's a 2G phone, and finding a carrier that still operates its old 2G network is becoming more difficult with each passing year. Then there's the phone's browser, which Nokia never updated to work with any internet security protocols beyond TLS 1.0. After Apple, Google, Microsoft and Mozilla came together to deprecate the standard in 2020, less than an estimated 25 percent of all websites support TSL 1.0 connections as of mid last year, meaning the 6300 essentially can't access the modern internet.
