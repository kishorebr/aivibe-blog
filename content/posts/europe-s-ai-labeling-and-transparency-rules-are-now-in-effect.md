<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The European Union has ushered in some additional rules that aim to make it easier for people to identify chatbots and AI deepfakes online. The <a href="https://commission.europa.eu/news-and-media/news/safer-and-more-transparent-ai-2026-08-02_en">new transparency obligations</a> under the bloc's landmark AI Act came into effect <a href="https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_26_1714">on August 2nd</a>, requiring companies to disclose when people are interacting with AI models, and if content has been generated or altered by them.</p>
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The <a href="https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-50">transparency rules</a> differ between providers (companies that develop and market AI systems) and deployers (platforms and services that use those AI systems), though some companies like Meta and SpaceXAI are classified as both.</p>
