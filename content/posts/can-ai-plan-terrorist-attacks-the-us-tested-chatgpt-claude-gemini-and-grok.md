ZDNET’s key takeaways




Investigators bypassed safeguards on four major AI platforms.



Experts confirmed viable attack information, despite AI errors.



Public information can become dangerous when AI organizes it at speed.








The Government Accountability Office (GAO) conducted a series of tests earlier this year designed to determine whether users could bypass guardrails and convince AIs to plan terrorist attacks. The investigators succeeded. In October, the agency published a report titled “Covert Testing Used Common Platforms to Create Viable Attack Plans,” which was released to the public in November.



The tests were conducted using Claude, Gemini, ChatGPT, and Grok. All four AIs produced attack scenarios that subject matter experts were able to confirm would have mostly succeeded.

In general, the details provided by the four AI platforms are not novel. Much of the information is readily available on the internet. But these plans were generated in hours, rather than weeks or months. AI could also surface ideas or methods that may not have been previously considered.



To the companies’ credit, their safeguards have continued to evolve since we conducted these tests. The companies also assure us that this is an area of concern and ongoing research. However, our report concludes that, in its current state, this technology can be dangerous, and its misuse represents a growing risk to our nation.




The Government Accountability Office is an independent, nonpartisan agency that works for the legislative branch of the US government.
